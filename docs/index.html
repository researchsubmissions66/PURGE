The Methodology

Traditional adversarial attacks perturb pixels at inference time to fool frozen models. PURGE operates at the dataset level, acting as a Targeted Latent-Space Poisoning Attack to blind downstream training.

Linear Concept Erasure: LEACE vs SPLINCE

We evaluate the efficacy of current state-of-the-art linear concept erasure methods—LEACE and SPLINCE—at removing the concept of Prostate ISUP grading (Targeted Erasure) while preserving un-targeted performance on Breast Tumor subtyping. The projectors were mathematically verified to perfectly zero-out the linear components of the target dataset.

Dataset Method Targeted Erasure (PANDA) AUC ⬇️ Preserved (BRACS) AUC ⬆️
Baseline (No Erasure) ABMIL (Hoptimus0) 0.9493 ± 0.0013 0.9482 ± 0.0168
LEACE Orthogonal Erasure 0.9453 ± 0.0015 (Failed) 0.9470 ± 0.0183
SPLINCE Oblique Erasure 0.9438 ± 0.0027 (Failed) 0.9448 ± 0.0183
Finding, pending re-run: neither method moved the target AUC (0.949 → ~0.944) while control performance was untouched. The natural reading is that a non-linear MIL model over patch embeddings routes around a linear subspace erasure. That conclusion is not yet safe: these projectors were applied as x P⊤ + μ rather than (x − μ) P⊤ + μ, leaving a residual offset of μP⊤ that corrupts the erasure. The comparison is being re-run with the corrected convention before any claim is made about the sufficiency of linear concept erasure.

Ablation Studies & Sweeps

To prove that targeted representational geometry erasure is the only effective way to blindly poison downstream tasks, we conducted two exhaustive control sweeps: one testing naive noise resilience, and another testing brute-force variance deletion.

Naive Noise & Dropout Sweep

Do Foundation Models rely on overall variance, or specific geometric structures? We injected massive Gaussian noise and dropped up to 90% of the features to test ABMIL's resilience.

Perturbation PANDA (Prostate) BRACS (Breast)
Baseline 0.949 0.943
Gaussian (σ=1.0) 0.951 0.946
Dropout (p=0.9) 0.940 0.944
Interpretation: ABMIL is highly robust to undirected perturbation. Dropping 90% of feature dimensions per patch costs under 0.01 AUC, and the small drops are proportional across datasets. This is the control that establishes what non-selective corruption looks like: any credible attack must do something a noise baseline cannot.

SVD K-Sweep

Can we brute-force the erasure by deleting the top K principal components of the target dataset? We targeted Prostate and swept K up to 500.

Components Deleted Target (PANDA) Collateral (BRACS)
Baseline (K=0) 0.949 0.943
K = 100 0.928 0.922
K = 200 0.918 0.925
K = 500 0.910 0.916
Interpretation: Deleting variance in bulk is not erasure. Removing 500 components leaves the target at 0.91 while costing the un-targeted breast task an almost identical amount (0.943 → 0.916) — damage without selectivity. The subspace is chosen by the target cohort's own variance, and those directions are largely shared across cohorts, which is why the collateral tracks the target so closely. Selecting the subspace discriminatively is the open question this motivates.
