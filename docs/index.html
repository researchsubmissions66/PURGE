The Central Finding

A linear probe on a pathology foundation model scores 0.95 on grading or subtyping, and the field reads that as evidence the model encodes the concept. We show that what the probe reads is largely a low-dimensional subspace specific to the cohort it was estimated on. Delete that subspace and the task collapses to chance. Fit the same deletion on a different cohort of the same organ and nothing happens.

Read the breast rows together. BACH and BRACS are both breast cohorts. A subspace encoding a concept of breast atypia should transfer between them. It does not — the BACH-fitted eraser leaves BRACS atypia at 0.898, essentially untouched. Worse, the five weakest configurations in the entire sweep are cross-cohort runs in which the target gains accuracy while collateral damage to control tasks rises to roughly +0.09. Fitting on the wrong cohort harms the bystanders more than the intended target. What the projection removes is the geometry of one cohort's feature distribution, not a portable clinical concept.

Method

Fit the target cohort's top-k principal directions on the training split, then project them out mean-preservingly. The transform is applied to train and test for every task, so the victim never sees clean features — a poisoning threat model, not evasion.

Affine null-space projection

z′ = (z − μ) − ((z − μ)U)U⊤ + μ

The projector is rank-deficient by construction, which is the whole point: an invertible map cannot remove information, however convincingly it fools a fixed probe. Every eraser in this work passes an automated invertibility audit before any result derived from it is reported.

Evaluation protocol

  • Patient-grouped 5-fold splits; all slides from one patient stay on one side.
  • The outer fold is the test set. Early stopping watches a separate inner split, never the reported one.
  • Probes are retrained from scratch on erased features. A probe co-adapted in the loop is not evidence.
  • Chance is 0.50 for macro one-vs-rest AUC regardless of class count.
  • An invertible low-rank adapter is carried throughout as a negative control that must fail to erase.

Interpretation: Below roughly 100 slides nothing happens. Most of the available effect arrives between 500 and 1000. Past that the cohorts themselves run out. Independently, larger training cohorts are harder to erase (r = +0.80 between training-set size and surviving AUC) — the same fact seen from the other side.

Interpretation: Undirected corruption is not erasure — noise and dropout damage everything roughly equally, which is precisely what a credible attack must beat. The invertible adapter moves the target by 0.003 on average, confirming the evaluation harness cannot be fooled by a transform that removes nothing. Beyond these, eleven further approaches were tested and failed, including Fisher and HSIC direction ranking, iterative INLP, adversarial erasers, optimal-transport erasure, and reconstruction bottlenecks. A control-aware spectral objective that prices collateral into the selection was also tested: it strictly weakens the attack, moving BRACS from 0.583 at λ = 0 to 0.856 at λ = 4.

In Progress

Does attention-based MIL resist?

Results above are slide-level probes, which are exactly equivalent to mean-pooled MIL because erasure and mean pooling are both linear and commute. Attention-based architectures re-learn which patches to weight after erasure and may route around it.

ABMIL, TransMIL and MeanMIL are being run across 3 cohorts × 5 folds. Mean-pooled MIL should reproduce the probe numbers exactly, which gives the comparison a built-in correctness check. This determines whether the work reports a practical attack or a methodological result about probe-based capability claims.

What is in the removed subspace?

Two analyses are running. First, pairwise principal angles between the subspaces fitted on each of six cohorts, which turns cohort-specificity into a direct measurement rather than an inference from the transfer table.

Second, each slide vector is split into the deleted coordinates and the surviving complement, and each half is asked what it predicts: the clinical label, or which cohort the slide came from. If the deleted half carries cohort identity as strongly as it carries the label, that is the mechanism behind the transfer failure.
