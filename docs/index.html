The Central Finding

A linear probe on a pathology foundation model scores 0.95 on grading or subtyping, and the field reads that as evidence the model encodes the concept. We show that what the probe reads is largely a low-dimensional subspace specific to the cohort it was estimated on. Delete that subspace and the task collapses to chance. Fit the same deletion on a different cohort of the same organ and nothing happens.

Method

Fit the target cohort's top-k principal directions on the training split, then project them out mean-preservingly. The transform is applied to train and test for every task, so the victim never sees clean features. This is a poisoning threat model, not evasion.

Affine null-space projection

z′ = (z − μ) − ((z − μ)U)U⊤ + μ

The projector is rank-deficient by construction, which is the whole point: an invertible map cannot remove information, however convincingly it fools a fixed probe. Every eraser in this work passes an automated invertibility audit before any result derived from it is reported.

Evaluation protocol

  • Patient-grouped 5-fold splits; all slides from one patient stay on one side.
  • The outer fold is the test set. Early stopping watches a separate inner split, never the reported one.
  • Probes are retrained from scratch on erased features. A probe co-adapted in the loop is not evidence.
  • Chance is 0.50 for macro one-vs-rest AUC regardless of class count.
  • An invertible low-rank adapter is carried throughout as a negative control that must fail to erase.

Mathematical Framework

The attack is a single rank-deficient affine map. Everything below follows from that, including the constraints that limit it.

Construction

Setup. An encoder \(E\) maps a tissue patch to \(z \in \mathbb{R}^{d}\). For a cohort \(\mathcal{C}\) with \(n\) slides, stack the slide-level representations as \(Z \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}\) with mean \(\mu\) and covariance \(\Sigma\).

Choice of subspace. We take the \(k\) directions along which the target cohort varies most, the maximiser of a trace objective over the Stiefel manifold:

$$U^\star \;=\; \arg\max_{U^\top U = I_k} \operatorname{tr}\!\left(U^\top \Sigma\, U\right) \;=\; \big[u_1,\dots,u_k\big],\qquad \Sigma u_i = \lambda_i u_i,\ \ \lambda_1 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_d$$

The eraser. Project that subspace out while preserving the cohort mean, so the transform is affine rather than merely linear:

$$z' \;=\; P\,(z-\mu) + \mu \;=\; Pz + b, \qquad P = I_d - UU^\top,\qquad b = \mu - P\mu = UU^\top\!\mu$$

What this does. For every input, the coordinates along the erased subspace collapse to one constant:

$$U^\top z' \;=\; U^\top\!\big(z - UU^\top z\big) + U^\top UU^\top\!\mu \;=\; U^\top\!\mu \qquad\text{for all } z$$

Those \(k\) coordinates have zero variance afterwards, so they carry no information about anything. Note the residual is \(U^\top\!\mu\), not zero, so a nonzero projection of the erased features onto \(U\) is expected and is not evidence that the erasure failed.

Why rank deficiency is required

\(\operatorname{rank}(P) = d-k < d\). This is not incidental. If a candidate eraser \(g\) were invertible, then for any label \(Y\),

$$I\big(g(Z);Y\big) = I(Z;Y)$$

and no information is removed, however convincingly the map defeats one fixed probe. An earlier version of this work used a residual adapter \(z' = z + \alpha BAz\), which is invertible for small \(\alpha\); it appeared to erase only because the probe was never retrained. Every eraser here is checked for rank deficiency before any result derived from it is reported, and an invertible adapter is carried as a negative control that must fail.

Commutation with mean pooling

For a bag of patches \(\{z_1,\dots,z_m\}\), erasing and averaging commute:

$$\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j} \big(Pz_j + b\big) \;=\; P\Big(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j} z_j\Big) + b$$

So a probe on erased slide means is exactly mean-pooled MIL on erased patches. This makes the cheap slide-level sweep an exact proxy for one MIL architecture, and gives the MIL experiments a built-in correctness check. It also fails for attention pooling, whose weights are a nonlinear function of the patches and are re-learned after erasure. That is precisely why attention must be measured separately.

The budget bound

\(\Sigma\) estimated from \(n\) samples has rank at most \(n-1\), so the attacker cannot remove more directions than they have slides:

$$k_{\text{eff}} = \min\big(k,\ n-1,\ d\big)$$

Attack strength is therefore capped by the attacker's data. Since chance-level erasure requires \(k \approx 512\), it requires at least 513 slides from the target cohort, and in practice more, because estimates near the sample limit are noisy.

Comparing two cohorts' subspaces

Fit \(U_A\) on cohort \(A\) and \(U_B\) on cohort \(B\). Their principal angles are the singular values of the overlap matrix:

$$\cos\theta_i = \sigma_i\big(U_A^\top U_B\big), \qquad \overline{\cos\theta} = \tfrac{1}{k}\textstyle\sum_i \sigma_i$$

If the subspace encoded a clinical concept, two cohorts of the same organ would give \(\overline{\cos\theta}\approx 1\). The transfer results say otherwise, which is the central claim of this work stated geometrically.

A control-aware generalisation

The objective above never sees the control tasks, which is where collateral damage comes from. Pricing them in gives a one-parameter family whose optimum is the top-\(k\) eigenvectors of a covariance pencil:

$$\mathcal{L}_\lambda(U) = \operatorname{tr}\Big(U^\top\big(\Sigma_{t} - \lambda\,\overline{\Sigma}_{c}\big)U\Big), \qquad \overline{\Sigma}_{c} = \tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\textstyle\sum_{c}\Sigma_{c}$$

A difference rather than a ratio: the Rayleigh form \(u^\top\Sigma_t u / u^\top\Sigma_c u\) needs \(\Sigma_c^{-1}\), which explodes along the null space when \(n \ll d\). Setting \(\lambda=0\) recovers plain projection exactly, and this is used as a validation check: the two agree to four decimal places on all three targets. Empirically \(\lambda>0\) strictly weakens the attack (BRACS 0.583 at \(\lambda=0\) rising to 0.856 at \(\lambda=4\)): the directions carrying target signal are largely the ones the controls also occupy, so subtracting control variance removes exactly what the attack needs.

Beyond these, eleven further approaches were tested and failed, including Fisher and HSIC direction ranking, iterative INLP, adversarial erasers, optimal-transport erasure, and reconstruction bottlenecks. A control-aware spectral objective that prices collateral into the selection was also tested: it strictly weakens the attack, moving BRACS from 0.583 at λ = 0 to 0.856 at λ = 4.

In Progress

Does attention-based MIL resist?

Results above are slide-level probes, which are exactly equivalent to mean-pooled MIL because erasure and mean pooling are both linear and commute. Attention-based architectures re-learn which patches to weight after erasure and may route around it.

ABMIL, TransMIL and MeanMIL are being run across 3 cohorts × 5 folds. Mean-pooled MIL should reproduce the probe numbers exactly, which gives the comparison a built-in correctness check. This determines whether the work reports a practical attack or a methodological result about probe-based capability claims.

What is in the removed subspace?

Two analyses are running. First, pairwise principal angles between the subspaces fitted on each of six cohorts, which turns cohort-specificity into a direct measurement rather than an inference from the transfer table.

Second, each slide vector is split into the deleted coordinates and the surviving complement, and each half is asked what it predicts: the clinical label, or which cohort the slide came from. If the deleted half carries cohort identity as strongly as it carries the label, that is the mechanism behind the transfer failure.
